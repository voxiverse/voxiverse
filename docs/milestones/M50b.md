# M50b: entity replication v2 (quantized motion, rates by distance, the jitter buffer)

**Why.** `Entity_Motion` and `Player_Motion` travel exactly (`[3]f64` sent whole on a
change, 24 bytes of position), the snapshot lane's budget is 8 parts of 1 150 bytes a
tick, and `MAX_KNOWN_ENTITIES` is 256. 2 000 entities in view cannot fit. The client
draws everyone a fixed `INTERPOLATION_TICKS` (3) behind the newest snapshot and has no
idea how fast anything moves, so a lost or deferred update is a stutter.

**Design (M50b).**

*Quantized motion* (`common/game/state_kinds.odin`; M39 said quantization waits for this):

- `Entity_Motion {x, y, z: i64, vx, vy, vz: i16, yaw, pitch: u8, looks: u16}`:
  position in 1/256 block (`i64(math.round(p * 256))`), velocity in 1/256 block per tick
  (clamped to ±32 767), yaw as `u8(round(yaw / TAU * 256) & 255)`, pitch mapped
  -π/2..π/2 to 0..255. Separate scalar fields, so a change sends only the axes that moved,
  each as M39's zigzag difference: a walking sheep costs head, key and one byte for each
  of x and z.
- `Player_Motion` the same, plus `flying, crouched`.
- `Own_Player` stays exact (prediction needs it).
- Quantizing is the put's job (`entities_put`, `players_put`); the simulation is untouched,
  and the roots do not change in this milestone.

*Rates by distance.* `State_Kind_Info` gains `rates: [4]Rate {within: i32 (blocks), every:
u8}`. A changed snapshot record is eligible when its `waiting + 1 >= every` for its
distance tier; among eligible ones M39's priority (waiting, kind priority, distance,
key) decides. `Entity_Motion`: within 16 every tick, 32 every 2, 64 every 4, beyond every
8; `Player_Motion`: 32 every tick, 64 every 2, beyond every 4. Integers only, so
deterministic. `MAX_KNOWN_ENTITIES` becomes 2 048; `SNAPSHOT_PARTS` stays 8.

*The jitter buffer* (`client/interp.odin`, replacing `Track` and `render_tick` in
`client/players.odin`):

- A `Track` keeps 16 snapshots `{tick, position, velocity, yaw, pitch}` (dequantized).
- `Remote_Clock`: on each completed snapshot of server tick T arriving at local time `now`,
  a sample `now - T / TICK_RATE` goes into a ring of 64. `base` is the ring's minimum;
  `spread` its 90th percentile minus `base`. The delay is `clamp(spread * TICK_RATE + 1.5,
  1.5, 8)` ticks. Each frame the render time moves toward `(now - base) * TICK_RATE -
  delay` at between 0.9 and 1.1 times real time (never backwards); more than 8 ticks off,
  it jumps (after a stall, a travel, a join).
- A view between two snapshots is linear; past the newest, it extrapolates with the
  newest velocity for at most 6 ticks, then holds. When a snapshot arrives that
  disagrees with what was drawn, the difference is kept as a correction that decays with
  a time constant of 100 ms, so nothing jumps. Players and entities use the same clock.
- F3's network panel shows the delay and the spread.

*Two buffers, one job each (decided with #30, 2026-10-27).* The **server** keeps an input
buffer per client (`game.Input_Queue`: one input applied per tick, depth from the client's
arrival jitter, catch-up at most one extra input per tick from a budget of idle ticks,
oldest inputs discarded only after a stall of about 0.4 s; see `common/game/prediction.odin`).
It decides how the *mover's* simulation advances, so a player's authoritative position
moves at most a tick (two in a logged catch-up) per tick for every watcher. The **client's**
`Remote_Clock` and tracks (above; the render clock of #29 is its first version, in
`client/players.odin`) decide how *watchers* draw what the server says. They do not
overlap: the client buffer must not try to smooth bunched inputs (the server no longer
produces them), only network jitter of snapshots. Build `Remote_Clock` on the existing
render clock rather than again; the walk-off test's 0.5-block step bound
(`test_still_player_stays_visible`) must keep holding, with both buffers' delays added.

**The order of the steps (M50b):** quantized kinds and their round trips; rates; the
client's clock and tracks with the synthetic test; the UDP and load tests; the bandwidth
baseline re-recorded (bytes less than before).

**Left out (M50b):** per-client bandwidth limits and reliable-lane budgets (M55); lag
compensation (M55); client-side prediction of entities; levels of detail for distant
entities' drawing; compressing whole snapshots.

**Tests (M50b):**

- `common/game/state_kinds_test.odin`: quantization round-trips within half a unit; yaw wraps the
  short way; a walking sheep's change costs at most 6 bytes; a still one sends nothing.
- `common/game/delta_test.odin`: rates: with 2 000 changing records spread over 0-64 blocks, a
  record within 16 is sent every tick it changes, every record within 8 ticks, and no
  snapshot over `SNAPSHOT_PARTS`.
- `client/interp_test.odin`, `test_smooth_under_loss`: a synthetic server (an entity on a
  circle of radius 10 at 4.2 b/s, snapshots by the rate for 40 blocks away) through a
  seeded link of 50 ms latency, ±20 ms jitter and 10% loss, drawn at 60 frames a second
  for 60 s: the drawn position stays within 0.15 blocks of the true one at the render
  time, and no frame moves it more than twice the true per-frame distance plus 0.01.
  Without loss the error stays under 0.02.
- `server/net_test.odin`, extended: in `test_players_through_ten_percent_loss`, 200 summoned
  sheep; within 30 ticks after the loss stops, every `Entity_Motion` the client holds
  equals the server's quantized value; during the loss no sheep the client draws (through
  the real tracks) moves more than 0.5 blocks between two frames.
- `server/entity_load_test.odin`, `test_two_thousand_in_bandwidth`: M50's 2 000 sheep, the
  mirror at the centre, ticks 300-900 counted by the pipe: server-to-client under 160 KB/s
  on average (5 461 bytes a tick), every sheep's motion record received at least once
  every 8 ticks while it moves, and the slowest tick still in M50's budget.

**Exit check (M50b):** `test_two_thousand_in_bandwidth` (2 000 entities in view within
tick and bandwidth budgets), `test_smooth_under_loss` and the extended UDP loss test
(smooth motion under 10% loss) and M50's churn test pass; the bandwidth tests pass against
the re-recorded baseline with fewer bytes than before; `build determinism` with M50's
roots unchanged; the suite and CI green.

**Check by hand (M50b):** a LAN game with `net_loss 10` on the joining client: sheep and the
other player move smoothly; F3 shows the delay settle near 2 ticks without loss.
