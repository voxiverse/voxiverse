# M50b: entity replication v2, part one (quantized motion with velocity, rates by distance, 2 000 entities in view)

**Why.** `Entity_Motion` and `Player_Motion` travel exactly (`common/game/state_kinds.odin`:
`position: [3]f64`, a scalar array the M39 codec writes whole on any change: 24 bytes of
position, plus two `f32` angles), the snapshot lane's budget is `SNAPSHOT_PARTS` 8 parts of
`SNAPSHOT_PART_BYTES` 1 150 (`common/game/replica.odin`, `flush_snapshot`: 7 600 bytes after
the 200-byte headroom a part), and `MAX_KNOWN_ENTITIES` is 256. 2 000 entities in view cannot
fit. Every changed record is sent every tick to everyone, however far.

**The split (decided 2026-10-30, #85).** Replication v2 is two rows: **M50b** (this row) is
the wire: quantized kinds with velocity, rates by distance, 2 048 known entities, and what the
client must do so rates do not freeze far things; **[M50g](M50g.md)** is the client's jitter
buffer (the adaptive clock, extrapolation with corrections), lag compensation told the delay
the client really draws at, and the smoothness tests under loss. Each has its own exit check.

**What this builds on (checked against the code 2026-10-30, #85):** M39's state model
(`common/game/schema.odin`'s `State_Kind_Info`, `delta.odin`'s `leaf_write_delta`: an
integer field's change is the zigzag varint of its difference from the client's acknowledged
value, a scalar array is written whole; `replica.odin`'s `flush_snapshot`: candidates sorted
by `sort_by_priority` (waiting, kind priority, squared distance in whole blocks from the
client's `origin`, kind, key), packed into the budget, unsent ones' `waiting` raised);
`server/entities.odin`'s `entities_put` and `server/players.odin`'s `players_put` (the
puts); `client/state_apply.odin` (`apply_entity_motion`, `apply_player_motion`,
`entity_snapshot`, `other_snapshot`); `client/players.odin` (`Track`, `track_add`,
`track_hold`, `track_view`, `Render_Clock`); the bandwidth baseline
(`server/bandwidth_test.odin`, `assets/baselines/net/bandwidth.txt`, `test_bandwidth_accept`);
M50e's entity world (`Kind_Id`, `Entity_Motion.looks: u16`, `entities_put` nearest first
through `spatial_cylinder`, the `budget_entities` scene); Q2's `net.state` fuzz target
(before this row in the order). The session first checks M50e as built and corrects this spec
in the same commit.

## Design (M50b)

### Quantized kinds (`common/game/state_kinds.odin`; M39 said quantization waits for this)

- `Entity_Motion {x, y, z: i64, vx, vy, vz: i16, yaw, pitch: u8, looks: u16}`: position in
  1/256 block (`QUANTUM :: 256`, `i64(math.round(p * QUANTUM))`), velocity in 1/256 block a
  tick (clamped to ±32 767: 4 266 blocks a second, past anything that moves), yaw as
  `u8(i64(math.round(yaw / TAU * 256)) & 255)`, pitch mapped -π/2..π/2 to 0..255. Separate
  scalar fields, so a change sends only the fields that moved, each as M39's zigzag
  difference from the client's acknowledged value: a sheep walking along x costs the entry's
  head, key and mask and one or two bytes for x (its velocity unchanged sends nothing).
- `Player_Motion` the same with `yaw, pitch: u16` (a player's look is read by other players:
  0.0055 degrees, not 1.4) plus `flying, crouched`.
- `Own_Player` stays exact (prediction needs it); nothing in the simulation is quantized.
  The roots do not change in this row (`build determinism` with M50f's roots).
- One place converts each way: `motion_quantize(position: [3]f64, velocity: [3]f32, yaw,
  pitch: f32) -> Quantized` and `motion_position(m) -> [3]f64`, `motion_velocity(m) ->
  [3]f32` (blocks a second), `motion_yaw`, `motion_pitch`, in `state_kinds.odin`. The puts
  call the first; `client/state_apply.odin`, `locate_entity_motion`,
  `locate_player_motion`, `dedicated/bench.odin`'s bots and the test clients
  (`server/net_test.odin`'s `Net_Client`, the mirror in `determinism_test.odin`) call the
  others. No other code reads the integer fields.
- `check_entity_motion` and `check_player_motion` check the integers: the position within
  `position_ok`'s range once dequantized, the pitch byte within 0..255 (always), nothing
  else to refuse (every bit pattern of the others is a value).
- `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 36 goes up; the schema hash changes; `build schema` regenerates the
  dissector (`tools/wireshark`); the kinds' `version` stays (the types changed, so the hash
  already refuses an old peer).

### Rates by distance (`common/game/schema.odin`, `replica.odin`)

- `State_Kind_Info` gains `rates: [4]Rate`, `Rate :: struct {within: i32 (blocks; 0: the
  rest), every: u8}`, read only for the snapshot lane with a `locate`. A **changed** record is
  eligible at a tick when its `waiting + 1 >= every` for its tier (by `Candidate.distance`,
  the squared whole-block distance `flush_snapshot` already computes, against `within *
  within`); creates and removes are always eligible. Ineligible candidates are not sorted or
  packed; their `waiting` is raised as an unsent one's is. Among eligible ones M39's priority
  decides, unchanged. Integers only, no clock: deterministic.
- `Entity_Motion`: within 16 every tick, within 32 every 2, within 64 every 4, beyond every 8.
  `Player_Motion`: every tick at every distance (today's), so nothing a player sees of other
  players changes in this row; whether far players may be sent less often is open question 1.
- `MAX_KNOWN_ENTITIES` becomes 2 048 (`Entity` and `Entity_Motion`'s `max_records`);
  `SNAPSHOT_PARTS` stays 8. M50e's `entities_put` already puts the nearest first.
- `flush_snapshot` today sorts every candidate (`slice.sort_by` over an index array in the
  temp allocator). With 2 048 records it keeps that shape but sorts only the eligible ones;
  the `budget_snapshots` scene below holds its cost.

### What the client does about records it was not sent

Since M39, a record present in the client's replica and absent from a complete snapshot was
unchanged, and `track_hold` (`client/players.odin`, `client/session_net.odin`) adds a sample
at the new tick with the old values: a still thing stays put, and when it moves again it does
not glide in from where it was last heard of (#27). With rates, absent also means *deferred*:
a far sheep walking along is sent every fourth tick, and holding it would draw it stopping
and jumping. The client cannot tell the two apart without bytes on the wire, so it does not
try: **`track_hold` becomes `track_continue`**: the held sample is the newest real one
carried on by its velocity (`motion_velocity`, zero for a still thing, so a still thing is
held exactly as today), for at most `CONTINUE_TICKS :: 10` ticks past the newest real
sample (the slowest tier's 8 and 2 more), then held. A carried sample is marked `guess`;
when a real sample arrives, the guesses after the previous real sample are dropped and the
real one goes in (`track_add` keeps its tick order). A real change that a guess missed (a
sheep that stopped while deferred) shows as a step back to the truth; M50g's corrections
make that step a decaying offset. `Snapshot` (`client/players.odin`) gains `velocity:
[3]f32` and `guess: bool`; `SNAPSHOTS` 8 becomes 16 (a far tier's real samples are 8 ticks
apart).

## The order of the steps (M50b)

1. `motion_quantize` and the readers; the quantized kinds; the round-trip and size tests.
2. `Rate` and the eligibility in `flush_snapshot`; `MAX_KNOWN_ENTITIES` 2 048; the rate tests.
3. `track_continue`, `Snapshot.velocity` and `guess`; the track tests; `still_test.odin`
   unchanged and passing.
4. `test_two_thousand_in_bandwidth`, the `budget_snapshots` scene, the memory test.
5. The bandwidth baseline re-recorded (`build test` with `-define:VX_BANDWIDTH_ACCEPT=true
   -define:ODIN_TEST_NAMES=server.test_bandwidth_accept`), both scenarios' bytes no more
   than before, quoted in the commit and the report.

This row is already a part of a split: it is not split again.

## Left out (M50b)

The adaptive clock, extrapolation with corrections, the claimed delay for lag compensation,
the smoothness tests under loss and F3's delay (M50g); per-client bandwidth limits and the
reliable lane's budget (M55); client-side prediction of entities; levels of detail for far
entities' drawing; compressing whole snapshots; quantizing `Own_Player`.

## Tests (M50b)

- `common/game/state_kinds_test.odin`:
  - `test_motion_quantize_round_trip`: 100 000 random positions (within ±2^24 blocks),
    velocities and angles (seeded `base.Rng`): dequantized within half a quantum (1/512
    block, 1/512 block a tick, half an angle step), yaw compared the short way round;
    quantizing a dequantized value gives the same integers (idempotent); velocities past the
    clamp clamp.
  - `test_motion_bytes`: through the real encoder (`write_entry` against a baseline value):
    a sheep moving 0.047 blocks a tick along x (1.4 b/s) costs at most 6 bytes as a change;
    one moving diagonally at most 8; a still one is no candidate at all (`value_diff` 0); a
    create costs at most 24.
  - `test_motion_check`: every pitch byte passes; a position dequantizing past `position_ok`
    is refused.
- `common/game/delta_test.odin` (the sender alone, a stepped list of records):
  - `test_rates_tiers`: 2 000 records changing every tick at distances spread over 0-96
    blocks with a budget large enough for all: a record within 16 is sent every tick, within
    32 every second tick, within 64 every fourth, beyond every eighth (exactly, from the
    `waiting` rule); creates and removes go the tick they happen.
  - `test_rates_under_budget`: the same 2 000 with the real budget: every record is sent at
    least once every 16 ticks (no starvation), no snapshot outgrows `SNAPSHOT_PARTS`, and the
    nearest tier is never starved by farther ones while its records fit.
  - `test_rates_deterministic`: two senders given the same records and ticks write identical
    bytes.
  - `test_still_records_cost_nothing`: 2 000 still records after their creates: the next 100
    snapshots carry no `Entity_Motion` entries.
- `client/players_test.odin` (new; the track alone, no session):
  - `test_continue_deferred`: real samples of a thing moving at a constant 4.2 b/s every 4
    ticks with its velocity, `track_continue` on the ticks between: the view at every
    quarter tick lies on the true line within 2/256 block.
  - `test_continue_still_holds`: velocity zero: the view is the held position exactly (the
    #27 behaviour).
  - `test_continue_stops`: no real sample for 30 ticks: the thing moves for `CONTINUE_TICKS`
    then holds, never further.
  - `test_guess_replaced`: a thing that stopped while deferred: when the real sample comes,
    the guesses after the previous real one are gone and the track holds the real position.
- `client/still_test.odin`: `test_still_player_stays_visible`, `test_still_sheep_stays_visible`
  and M50's `test_dropped_stack_stays_visible` unchanged in every assertion and passing.
- `server/entity_load_test.odin`, `test_two_thousand_in_bandwidth`: M50e's 2 000-sheep scene
  (`budget_entities`, called for what it does) with the mirror client of `determinism_test.odin`
  at the centre, server-to-client bytes counted by the pipe over ticks 300-900: on average at
  most 5 461 bytes a tick (160 KB/s at 30 Hz); every sheep the mirror knows has its motion
  record received at least once in every 8 ticks while it moves (the mirror compares its
  replica's value with the sender's for that tick); every sheep within `ENTITY_VIEW` is known
  (up to 2 048). Bytes are exact on a stepped server (as the bandwidth test's), so this is a
  test, not a timing.
- `server/entity_load_test.odin`, `test_snapshot_memory_flat`: the same scene, the server's
  sender and the mirror's receiver for the client measured by a tracking allocator: what
  they hold after tick 600 equals after tick 1 200 (flat), and is at most 8 MB each (the ring
  of 32 snapshots of 2 048 records).
- The bandwidth tests (`test_bandwidth_session`, `test_bandwidth_crowd`): pass against the
  re-recorded baseline, roots unchanged, server-to-client bytes fewer than the old baseline's
  in both scenarios.
- Fuzz: Q2's `net.state` generator writes the new kinds (it is built from the schema; the
  session checks it writes `Entity_Motion` changes with every field), its smoke target clean
  on the fixed seed, its regression files replayed on three OSes.
- Unchanged and passing: `server/net_test.odin`'s `test_players_through_ten_percent_loss`
  (its comparison is against the sender's recorded value, now the quantized one),
  `client/avatars_test.odin`, `client/world_test.odin`, `determinism_test.odin`.

## Benchmarks (M50b)

`budget_snapshots` in `server/budgets.odin`, judged by `build bench budgets -check` (metrics
in `common/base/metrics.odin`, limits in `tools/bench/budgets.odin`'s `BUDGET_LIMITS`, the median of
the repetitions): M50e's 2 000-sheep scene with one mirror client at the centre, 600 ticks;
the scene fails the run unless the mirror knows at least 1 900 sheep at the end.

| Metric | What | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| `Snapshot_Build` | mean CPU us for one client's snapshot (`flush_snapshot`: candidates, rates, sort, packing, the ring) | 1 500 |
| `Snapshot_Build_Max` | the slowest | 5 000 |
| `Snapshot_Apply` | mean CPU us for the mirror to receive and apply one complete snapshot | 1 500 |

The limits are several times a fast desktop's, as the other budgets are, and are set from the
first measured run in the session (the commit quotes the run): if the measured median is
over a fifth of a limit, the limit stays and the report says so; a limit is never raised to
pass.

## Open questions for the owner

1. Far players' update rate. This row keeps every other player's motion at every tick at any
   distance (today's). Players beyond 32 or 64 blocks could be sent every second or fourth
   tick like entities (less bandwidth with many players; far players drawn from fewer
   samples, which matters in long-range fights). Keep every tick, or rate them?

different places): stand among them, then walk 60 blocks away and look back: far sheep walk
without stopping and jumping (they may look less smooth until M50g), near ones exactly as
before; F3's net line (`client/dev_panels.odin`: "down ... KB/s") shows fewer bytes a second than
the previous build with the same flock.
