# M50g: entity replication v2, part two (the jitter buffer: an adaptive clock, extrapolation, corrections; lag compensation told the delay)

**Why.** Split from M50b (2026-10-30, #85). After M50b the wire carries quantized motion with
velocity at rates by distance, and the client carries a deferred record on by its velocity.
What it draws is still timed by `Render_Clock` (`client/players.odin`): the newest tick heard
plus the time since, minus a fixed `INTERPOLATION_TICKS` 3 (100 ms), whatever the network
does. On a quiet LAN that is 100 ms of needless delay; through jitter of more than 100 ms
everyone stutters; and a real change a guess missed shows as a step. The server's lag
compensation (`server/weapons.odin`'s `shooter_latency_ms`: round trip plus
`INTERPOLATION_TICKS`) assumes the fixed delay too, so once the client's delay adapts, shots
would be judged against the wrong past unless the client says what it drew.

**What this builds on (checked against the code 2026-10-30, #85):** M50b (`Snapshot.velocity`,
`guess`, `track_continue`, `CONTINUE_TICKS`, `motion_*`); `client/players.odin` (`Track`,
`track_add`, `track_view`, `Render_Clock`, `clock_target`, `clock_heard`, `clock_advance`,
`clock_live`, `STALE_SECONDS`, `lerp_angle`), `client/session_net.odin` (the snapshot's
arrival, `session_others_now`), `client/state_apply.odin`; the server's input buffer
(`game.Input_Queue`, `common/game/prediction.odin`, #30); lag compensation
(`server/rewind.odin`: `rewind_ticks`, `REWIND_MAX_TICKS`, `rewind_frame`;
`server/weapons.odin`: `shooter_latency_ms`, the `claimed_latency_ms` parameter the tests
use); the messages `Shoot` and `Fire` (`common/game/protocol.odin`); the simulated link
(`common/netcode` `Conditions {loss, duplicate, latency_ms, jitter_ms, seed}`, the client's
`net_latency_ms`, `net_jitter_ms`, `net_loss` settings); F3's net line
(`client/dev_panels.odin`). The session first checks M50b as built and corrects this spec in
the same commit.

## Design (M50g)

### Where the code lives

The tracks and the clock move out of `client/players.odin` into a package of their own,
**`client/interp`** (`interp.odin`; no session, no GPU, no window, as `client/mesh` is), so
the tests and `tools/bench` can run them alone. `client/players.odin` keeps `Other_Player`,
`Other_Entity` and the session's glue. (M50d's riding prediction uses the same clock.)

### Two buffers, one job each (decided with #30, 2026-10-27)

The **server** keeps an input buffer per client (`game.Input_Queue`): it decides how the
*mover's* simulation advances, so a player's authoritative position moves at most a tick (two
in a logged catch-up) per tick for every watcher. The **client's** clock and tracks decide how
*watchers* draw what the server says, and smooth only the network's jitter of snapshots, not
bunched inputs (the server no longer produces them). `Remote_Clock` is built on the existing
`Render_Clock` (its skew-limited advance and its snap), not again.

### `Remote_Clock`

- On each **complete** snapshot of server tick T arriving at local time `now` (seconds, the
  frames' clock as today's `since`, so tests drive it), a sample `now - T / TICK_RATE` goes
  into a ring of 64. `base` is the ring's minimum; `spread` its 90th percentile minus `base`
  (the ring sorted into a fixed scratch array: no allocation).
- The **delay** is `clamp(spread * TICK_RATE + 1.5, DELAY_MIN 1.5, DELAY_MAX 8)` ticks, and
  until 16 samples have come it is `INTERPOLATION_TICKS` (3, today's).
- The target is `(now - base) * TICK_RATE - delay`. Each frame the shown time moves toward it
  at between 0.9 and 1.1 times real time (`CLOCK_MAX_SKEW`, never backwards); more than
  `CLOCK_SNAP_TICKS` 10 off it jumps (after a stall, a travel, a join), as today.
- The minimum drifts if the two machines' clocks drift: samples older than the ring are
  forgotten, so `base` follows within 64 snapshots (about 2 s).
- `clock_live` keeps `STALE_SECONDS`.

### Views and corrections

- A view between two samples is linear in position and short-way in yaw (today's). Past the
  newest real sample it continues by the sample's velocity for at most `CONTINUE_TICKS`
  (M50b), then holds.
- **Corrections.** When a real sample arrives that disagrees with what was drawn for its tick
  (the guesses' position), the difference becomes the track's `correction` (a `[3]f64`), drawn
  added to the view and decaying as `exp(-dt / CORRECTION_TAU)`, `CORRECTION_TAU :: 0.1` s;
  a difference over `CORRECTION_SNAP` 4 blocks (a teleport) is not smoothed. So nothing the
  client draws jumps because of a late or deferred update.
- Players and entities share the clock and the tracks.
- F3's net line gains `delay %.1f t (spread %.1f)`.

### Lag compensation told the delay

`Shoot` and `Fire` gain `behind: u8`: the client's shown delay at the moment of the shot,
in 1/8 tick (newest tick heard minus the shown time, clamped 0..255). The server's
`shooter_latency_ms` becomes round trip plus `behind`, clamped to the clock's own range
(`DELAY_MIN`..`DELAY_MAX` ticks plus one tick of frame slack), so a client cannot claim to
see further into the past than any honest clock would; `rewind_ticks` and `REWIND_MAX_TICKS`
are unchanged (8 ticks plus a round trip stays within it, checked by a test).
`PROTOCOL_VERSION` goes up. The `claimed_latency_ms` test parameter stays.

## The order of the steps (M50g)

1. `client/interp`: the tracks moved there unchanged (`client/still_test.odin` and M50b's
   track tests pass, moved with them).
2. `Remote_Clock` and its tests; the session on it.
3. Corrections; the synthetic smoothness test.
4. `behind` in `Shoot` and `Fire`; lag compensation; its tests.
5. The loss test through real sessions; the `budget_tracks` metric; F3.

This row is already a part of a split: it is not split again.

## Left out (M50g)

Per-client bandwidth limits (M55); client-side prediction of entities (M50d predicts riding
only); levels of detail for far entities; a separate clock per world (one session, one
world at a time); adapting the server's input buffer (it is #30's).

## Tests (M50g)

- `client/interp/clock_test.odin`:
  - `test_clock_settles`: snapshots every tick with no jitter: the delay settles at 1.5 ticks
    within 64 snapshots; with seeded uniform ±20 ms jitter it equals, exactly, the delay a
    reference in the test computes from the same 64 samples (a sorted copy, the 90th
    percentile, the clamp), and lies between 1.5 and 2.9 ticks.
  - `test_clock_never_backwards`: 10 000 frames of random dt (1-50 ms) and random arrivals
    (seeded): the shown time never decreases and its rate stays within 0.9..1.1 of real time
    except at snaps; a stall of 1 s snaps once.
  - `test_clock_drift`: the server's clock 0.1% fast: the delay stays within 0.5 ticks of its
    no-drift value over 10 minutes of simulated frames.
  - `test_clock_no_alloc`: 10 000 frames under a panicking allocator (as M49b's interpreter
    test): nothing allocates.
- `client/interp/interp_test.odin`, **`test_smooth_under_loss`**: a synthetic server (an entity
  on a circle of radius 10 at 4.2 b/s; snapshots at the rate for 40 blocks away, quantized
  as M50b's `motion_quantize`) through a seeded link of 50 ms latency, ±20 ms jitter and 10%
  loss, drawn at 60 frames a second for 60 s: the drawn position (with its correction) stays
  within 0.15 blocks of the true one at the shown time; no frame moves it more than twice the
  true per-frame distance plus 0.01; without loss and jitter the error stays under 0.02.
  The link is the test's own (a seeded queue), not `netcode`: the test is about the clock and
  the tracks, deterministic and instant.
- `client/interp/interp_test.odin`, `test_correction_decays`: a sample 1 block off what was
  drawn: the drawn offset is under 0.37 blocks 0.1 s later and under 0.01 after 0.5 s, never
  overshooting; a 5-block difference is taken at once.
- `client/smooth_test.odin` (new; real sessions on a stepped server, as `still_test.odin`),
  `test_sheep_smooth_through_loss`: two sessions, the watcher's link with 10% loss, 10%
  duplicates, 15 ms latency and ±25 ms jitter (`test_players_through_ten_percent_loss`'s
  conditions), 200 sheep summoned 10-60 blocks from the watcher and walking, the mover
  walking a square; two frames of half a tick for each server tick: no sheep or player the
  watcher draws moves more than 0.5 blocks between two frames (teleports aside: none
  happen); within 30 ticks after the loss stops, every sheep the watcher draws is within
  1/64 block of the server's quantized value at the shown tick.
- `server/weapons_test.odin` (extended): `test_shot_uses_behind`: a shot claiming 2 ticks
  behind is judged against the frame 2 ticks plus the round trip back (a target that moved
  is hit where the shooter saw it); `test_behind_clamped`: a claim of 255/8 ticks is judged
  as `DELAY_MAX` plus one; `test_rewind_covers_the_delay`: `DELAY_MAX` plus one tick plus a
  300 ms round trip is within `REWIND_MAX_TICKS`.
- `common/game/protocol_test.odin`: `Shoot` and `Fire` round-trip with `behind`; Q2's
  `net.client_decode` generator gains the field (from the message's type), its smoke clean.
- `client/world_test.odin`: the assertion on today's clock (`c.time - (100 -
  INTERPOLATION_TICKS)`) tests a clock this row replaces: it becomes the same check on
  `Remote_Clock` before its 16th sample (where the delay is still `INTERPOLATION_TICKS`),
  and the report names the change.
- Unchanged and passing: `client/still_test.odin`, M50b's track tests (moved), the avatars
  tests, `server/net_test.odin`, the bandwidth tests (the bytes change only by `behind` in
  the session scenario's shots: re-recorded once if so, the report quoting both).

walking player with the pistol hits where the crosshair was on them.
