# M55: secure transport, rate limits, validation and lag compensation

**Why.** Since M27 the netcode has been authenticated at the door and plain on the wire. M27
accepted that for telemetry and wrote "if that changes, the cost is in the netcode, once,
for everything". With agent packs, accounts and economies, it changes now. Anyone on the
path can read chat, and a forged `Disconnect` with the visible token kicks a player.
Nothing proves the server is the one the player joined before, and an account proof can be
relayed. The server's defences against a hostile client are scattered: decode checks,
`INPUT_BUDGET`, `MAX_QUEUED_ACTIONS`, reach in `apply_edit` and `entity_reachable`,
`FIRE_EVERY`, chat tokens, M27's report windows, M31's inventory fuzz. Nothing is logged in
one place, and nothing adds up to a kick. The survey for this section found:

- `common/netcode/host.odin`: a stateless cookie handshake (`Connect` padded to 300 bytes,
  `Challenge`, `Response` with an HMAC password proof, `Accept` with a random token),
  `HANDSHAKES_PER_SECOND :: 200`. Peers are found by source address.
- `common/netcode/conn.odin`: `MAGIC "VXV1"`, `MTU 1200`, `DATA_HEADER` 19 bytes, `FRAGMENT_SIZE`
  1176, u16 sequence numbers, 32 bits of acks.
- The account proof (`common/game/account.odin`) signs `"voxiverse join v1" || nonce || account`,
  spoken in game messages after the netcode handshake. It is bound to nothing about the
  connection.
- Captures (`common/netcode/capture.odin`) are written by the socket, so they would hold ciphertext.
  The dissector (`tools/wireshark/voxiverse.lua`, `check.lua`) and the CI step "Multiplayer
  and Wireshark" read them.
- `Attack {entity, after}`: the server checks reach against the entity's *current* box
  while the client clicked on where it drew it, interpolated in the past.

**Design.**

*The handshake, net version 2.*

- `MAGIC :: 0x32565856` ("VXV2"), and the game's `PROTOCOL_VERSION` is bumped. A VXV1
  packet is ignored, as any stray one is.
- Keys come from `core:crypto`: `x25519`, `hkdf` (SHA-256), `chacha20poly1305`, `ed25519`,
  `sha2`. `core:crypto/noise` was considered and not taken. Its transport uses implicit
  nonces, which UDP loss and reordering break, and its handshake keeps state before the
  cookie proves the address.

| Packet | From | Contents | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connect | client | salt u64, client ephemeral X25519 public `ce` [32], net version u16; padded | 300 |
| Challenge | server | salt, cookie [16] = HMAC-SHA256(secret, address, port, salt, `ce`, window)[:16] | 29 |
| Response | client | salt, cookie, `ce`, password proof [16]; padded | 300 |
| Accept | server | salt, token u32, server ephemeral `se` [32], server identity key `sk` [32], signature [64], confirm tag [16] | 161 |
| Deny | server | salt, reason (unauthenticated, as today; only while handshaking) | ≤ 213 |

- The server keeps no state until a Response carries a good cookie, as today. Answers stay
  smaller than asks.
- On a good Response, the server:
  1. Makes `se`, computes `shared = X25519(se_priv, ce)`, and refuses an all-zero result
     (a low-order point) without answering.
  2. Computes `T = SHA-256("voxiverse transport v2" || salt || ce || se || sk || token ||
     cookie)`.
  3. Derives `keys = HKDF-SHA256(ikm = shared, salt = T, info = "voxiverse keys v2", 64)`:
     client-to-server key `keys[0:32]`, server-to-client key `keys[32:64]`.
  4. Signs `"voxiverse server v2" || T` with its identity key.
  5. Computes the confirm tag: the AEAD tag of an empty message under the
     server-to-client key at packet number 0.
  6. Stores the Accept bytes in the `Peer`, so a repeated Response (a lost Accept) gets the
     same Accept, as today's resend does.
- The channel binding is `B = SHA-256("voxiverse binding v2" || T)`, kept as `Conn.binding`.
- The password proof becomes `HMAC-SHA256(password, cookie || ce)[:16]`, bound to this
  handshake. It can still be guessed offline from a sniffed Response. That is accepted:
  passwords are a LAN convenience, and the account is the identity.
- The client checks, in order: the signature under `sk`; `sk` against what it pinned for this
  server; the confirm tag. A failure is `Failed` with a reason, and no data is sent.

*Server identity and pinning.*

- A universe has `<universe>/server.key` (M54's key file format, header
  `voxiverse-server-key 1`), made on first start. A server with no save directory (tests,
  scratch) makes one per run.
- The collector has `<dir>/collector.key`.
- `netcode.host_open` takes the identity (`^ed25519.Private_Key`). `link_connect` takes
  `pinned: Maybe([32]u8)` and reports `l.server_key`.
- The client keeps `known_servers.txt` beside its settings: `<host>:<port> <64 hex key>
  <first seen unix>`. It is trust on first use, as SSH does.
- A different key is refused with: "This server's identity changed since you last joined
  (now ab12..., was cd34...). If its owner reset or moved it, remove its line from
  known_servers.txt." LAN-discovered servers pin the same way. The uploader pins the
  collector in the same file.
- Singleplayer's in-process pipe has no transport and a zero binding.

*Accounts tied to the channel.*

- `account_sign(k, nonce, binding)` and `account_verify(account, nonce, binding, sig)` sign
  `"voxiverse join v2" || nonce || account || binding`.
- The server verifies with that peer's `host_conn(...).binding`. A man in the middle who
  relays the server's nonce to a victim gets a proof for the victim's channel, which fails
  on its own.
- The collector's proofs change the same way.

*Data packets.*

- The clear header is the AEAD's associated data: magic 4, kind 1, token 4, packet number
  `pn` u64 8 bytes, 17 bytes in all.
- Then, sealed: ack u16, ack bits u32, reliable ack u16, the records. Then the 16-byte tag.
- The nonce is `[4]u8{} || pn` (little-endian). The keys differ per direction, so the
  nonces may repeat across directions.
- `pn` starts at 1 in both directions (0 is the confirm tag). The reliability layer's u16
  sequence is `u16(pn)`, so `conn.odin`'s ack logic is unchanged.
- `DATA_HEADER` becomes 41 bytes (was 19) and `FRAGMENT_SIZE` 1154 (was 1176), 1.9% of a
  full packet. M39's snapshot parts follow `FRAGMENT_SIZE` by construction.
- `Disconnect` has the same clear header with a sealed reason. An unauthenticated one is
  ignored, so a forged kick no longer works.
- `Discover` and `Discover_Reply` stay plain: what a LAN server tells everyone is public.
- Receiving, in this order:
  1. The magic and kind.
  2. The peer by address, and the token.
  3. The replay window, as a cheap precheck.
  4. The AEAD open, in place.
  5. Then, and only then, `pn` marked in the window.
  6. `conn_packet` on the plaintext.
- The replay window is per direction per connection: the highest `pn` seen and 256 bits
  behind it. Refused: 0, anything 256 or more behind, and anything already marked.
- Counters in `Conn_Stats`: `auth_failed`, `replayed`, `too_old`. The old `duplicates`
  becomes `replayed`: the simulated `dup_percent` now lands there, and the conn tests that
  counted duplicates count replays.
- Sealing happens in `conn_flush`, in place, each packet grown by the tag. `Conn` always
  encrypts. There is no plaintext mode. `conn_test.odin` makes keys with
  `conn_keys_for_test(seed)`.
- Cost budget: crypto at most 3% of the server's tick time at M55b's 200-bot load. The new
  metric `base.Metric.Net_Crypto_Us` (sealing and opening time per tick) is added to the
  `server` bench scenario, so the figure is measured, not assumed.

*Debugging encrypted traffic.*

- The capture records plaintext.
  - Non-data packets (the handshake, discovery) are captured by the socket as today.
  - The socket no longer captures Data or Disconnect.
  - `host.odin` and `link.odin` capture them after opening (received) and before sealing
    (sent), with kind `Data_Plain` (`0x81`) or `Disconnect_Plain` (`0x87`). Those values are
    never valid on the wire. The packet keeps its clear header (`pn` included) and has no
    tag.
- So the dissector needs no crypto: Wireshark's Lua has none, and a key log would not help
  it. It learns VXV2, the header with `pn`, the plaintext kinds and the new handshake
  fields. `check.lua` keeps decoding every packet, and CI's tshark step is unchanged.
- A capture therefore holds everything said in clear. It is a debugging tool on a server
  one owns: the file is made with mode 0600 and DEBUGGING.md says so.
- `netdump` reads M39's in-process trace and is unaffected.

*Rate limits per message kind* (`server/limits.odin`, constants).

- A token bucket per playing client per kind, counted in ticks with integer milli-tokens.
- Over the limit, the message is dropped. An `Edit` gets the `Edit_Result` rejection, so the
  client rolls back. A violation is recorded (below).

| Kind | Per second | Burst | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input | 36 | 60 | 30 are due; `INPUT_BUDGET` still applies |
| Ping | 4 | 8 | |
| Teleport | 10 | 20 | permissioned too |
| Set_Rule | 5 | 20 | |
| Edit | 20 | 40 | creative breaks at about 5/s held |
| Command | 5 | 10 | |
| Attack | 20 | 20 | |
| Fire | 10 | 10 | `FIRE_EVERY` stays |
| Chat | 5 | 10 | M13's own tokens stay, with their message |
| Inventory_Request | 40 | 80 | drag-spreading sends bursts |
| Dial | 10 | 20 | |
| Use_Entity, Use_Block | 10 | 20 | each |
| Stats_Request | 2 | 4 | |
| Telemetry_Report | 1 | 4 | M27's window stays |
| Prediction_Check | 2 | 4 | one a second is sent |
| Hello, Proof, Choose | once | | a second is a disconnect, as today |

- Bytes from a peer, in netcode: 128 KiB/s, burst 256 KiB, counted before opening. Over that,
  packets are dropped and counted. Sustained over it for 2 s, the host raises
  `Peer_Flooding {peer}` as a `Host_Event`.

*The validation framework* (`server/validate.odin`).

- `Check :: enum u8 {Decode, Order, Rate, Bytes, Movement, Reach, Aim, Edit, Inventory,
  Action, Chat, Desync}`.
- `Violation :: enum u16`, append-only. `VIOLATION_INFO: [Violation]Violation_Info {name,
  check, weight: f32, text}` (`text` is what the kick tells the player).
- `violation(s, c, v, kind := Client_Kind(0), value: f64 = 0, limit: f64 = 0)` is the one
  call every check makes. It adds the weight to `c.score`, writes the reasons log, and kicks
  at the threshold.
- `c.score` decays by half every 60 s (per tick, × 0.5^(1/1800)). At 100 or more the client
  is disconnected with "refused: <text of the violation that crossed it>", and the line's
  `action` is `kicked`.
- The violations and their weights:
  - 100 (an instant kick): `Decode_Failed`, `Before_Hello`, `Unexpected_Handshake`,
    `Batch_To_Server` (today's `disconnect` calls, now through `violation`);
  - 2: `Rate_Exceeded` (with the kind), `Fire_Too_Fast`, `Aim_Off` (below; logged, never
    refuses);
  - 20: `Peer_Flooding`, `Snapshot_Ack_Future` (an `Input.snapshot` past the server's tick,
    ignored today), `Look_Invalid` (yaw or pitch NaN or out of range: decode catches some);
  - 1 a tick: `Input_Overrun` (the queue full, or inputs arriving faster than 1.2 a tick
    over 10 s: a sped-up client);
  - 1: `Input_Out_Of_Order`, `Rewind_Clamped` (below);
  - 10: `Fly_Not_Allowed` (an input asks to fly where the rules do not allow it), `Action_Queue_Full`;
  - 5: `Action_Ahead` (an action whose `after` is more than 60 ahead of the newest input
    received), `Reach_Edit`, `Reach_Entity`, `Reach_Block_Use`, `Inventory_Invalid` (a slot
    or action that cannot exist);
  - 3: `Chat_Flood` (M13's limiter refused a line);
  - 10 each beyond 3 a minute: `Desync_Repeated` (M38's detector).
- Not violations (weight 0, debug log only): an edit refused by the rules or because the
  chunk was unloaded, a late click on an entity that is gone. Honest players do these under
  latency.
- The reasons log is `<universe>/violations/YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl`, one line each: `{"t": <unix
  ms>, "tick": n, "account": "<hex>", "character": "...", "address": "...", "violation":
  "reach_edit", "check": "reach", "kind": "edit", "value": 7.9, "limit": 6.5, "weight": 5,
  "score": 23.5, "action": "dropped" | "logged" | "kicked"}`.
  - Addresses are kept because this is moderation data, not telemetry. `docs/telemetry.md`'s
    no-address rule is about the lake.
  - At most 30 lines per client per minute. The rest are counted and written as one line
    `{"violation": "suppressed", "count": n}`.
  - A server with no directory keeps the last 256 in memory, for the tests and the command.
  - `/violations [player] [n]` prints recent lines (`server.violations`).

*Lag compensation for hits.*

- Each world keeps `rewind: [REWIND_TICKS]Rewind_Frame` (`REWIND_TICKS :: 16`, 533 ms).
  Each frame holds the tick and the id, box low and box high of every hittable entity,
  pushed at the end of the world's tick.
- `Attack` gains `view_tick: u32` and `view_alpha: u8`: where the client's interpolation of
  remote entities stood when it clicked (M50's jitter buffer gives both). The client fills
  them in `client/session.odin`.
- The server's check:
  - `rewind = s.tick - view_tick`, clamped to `0..MAX_REWIND_TICKS` (12, 400 ms). A value
    out of range is clamped and gives `Rewind_Clamped`.
  - The entity's box is interpolated between the frames at `view_tick` and `view_tick + 1`
    by `view_alpha / 255`.
  - The hit is accepted if the player's eye, at the input the attack follows (as today), is
    within reach plus `EDIT_REACH_SLACK` of the rewound box or the current one.
  - Otherwise it is refused with `Reach_Entity`.
- Aim is checked, not enforced: the ray from the eye along that input's look must pass
  within 0.3 blocks of the rewound box, or `Aim_Off` is logged.
- Rockets (`Fire`) are not compensated: the server simulates them from the input tick's
  position already.
- Players are not targets: there is no attack on players yet.

*The hostile client* (`bot/hostile.odin`, a behaviour, and a raw attacker for transport
tests). Its script, one step every 30 ticks after joining, each step counted. Over the
network it sends:

- a truncated message;
- an unknown message kind;
- 100 inputs in one tick;
- an input with a NaN yaw;
- an input asking to fly without permission;
- 200 edits in a second;
- an edit 20 blocks away;
- an attack on an entity 30 blocks away;
- an attack with `view_tick` 2 seconds old;
- an inventory request on slot 9999;
- 60 chat lines in a second;
- an action 500 inputs ahead;
- an `Input.snapshot` from the future;
- a `Telemetry_Batch`.

The transport attacks come from a UDP proxy in the test (`common/netcode/proxy_test_helpers.odin`,
between a link and a host on loopback), which can:

- flip any one byte of a Data packet: in the header, the ciphertext or the tag;
- resend a captured packet a second time, and again 300 packets later;
- send a forged `Disconnect` with the right token but no key;
- send a Data packet with another connection's token;
- send a Response whose `ce` differs from its Connect's;
- send a Response with a low-order point;
- flood 1000 Connects a second.

**The order of the steps:**

1. The crypto in `conn.odin` (keys, seal and open, the replay window, the new header) with
   test keys, and `conn_test.odin` passing on it.
2. The handshake in `host.odin` and `link.odin`, server identity and pinning,
   `known_servers.txt`, the channel binding into the account proof, the collector.
   `host_test.odin`, `server/net_test.odin` and `collector_test.odin` pass.
3. Plaintext captures, the dissector, `check.lua`, DEBUGGING.md. CI's Wireshark step is
   checked locally with `lua5.4` if present.
4. `server/validate.odin`, the reasons log, `/violations`, today's scattered checks moved
   onto `violation`.
5. Rate limits and the netcode byte limit.
6. Lag compensation.
7. The hostile client, the proxy, their tests.

If the session must split, the remainder is M55c: steps 4 to 7, with the hostile-client test
as its exit check.

**Left out:**

- Address migration (a client whose NAT rebinds reconnects).
- Rekeying (a u64 packet number never runs out).
- Post-quantum key exchange.
- Encrypting LAN discovery.
- Protecting passwords from offline guessing.
- Bans that follow from violations (kicks only; bans stay the moderators').
- Players as hit targets.
- Anti-cheat on the client.

**Tests:**

- `common/netcode/conn_test.odin`, on test keys: every old test, plus:
  - a byte flipped anywhere, one test per region, is refused and counted in `auth_failed`,
    and the connection survives;
  - a replay within the window and one past it, counted;
  - packets reordered within 256 are accepted.
- `common/netcode/host_test.odin`:
  - handshake end to end;
  - a bad server signature, a wrong pinned key and a bad confirm tag each fail the link with
    their reasons;
  - a low-order point and a mismatched `ce` get no answer;
  - a forged Disconnect is ignored;
  - another connection's token is refused;
  - the Accept is resent after a lost one and is identical;
  - the 201st handshake in a second is not answered;
  - `Peer_Flooding` after 2 s over the byte limit.
- `server/net_test.odin`:
  - `test_account_proof_bound`: a relaying middle man (two links and a host) passes the
    server's nonce to a victim client and its proof back. The server refuses: "the
    account's proof is wrong".
  - The UDP suites (chat, items, gates, sheep, ...) pass unchanged on the encrypted
    transport.
- `client` test: `known_servers.txt` is written on first join. A changed key is refused
  with the message above. Removing the line lets the client join again.
- `server/validate_test.odin`:
  - each violation, made by hand, writes its line with the right fields;
  - the score decays by half in 1800 ticks;
  - 100 kicks, with the text;
  - suppression after 30 lines a minute.
- `server/limits_test.odin`: per kind, the burst passes and the next message is dropped;
  the rate refills; an `Edit` over the limit gets its rejection.
- `server/latency_test.odin`, extended, `test_honest_play_clean`: the M38 script at 200 ms
  with jitter, and the determinism session's script, both leave the score under 10 and
  write no violation of weight 5 or more.
- `server/lagcomp_test.odin`: a sheep moved at a steady 4 blocks/s (test-only velocity) and
  a client at 150 ms each way, interpolating. It attacks when its drawn sheep is at 0.2
  inside reach and the server's current sheep is outside it. With compensation the hit is
  accepted. With `view_tick` set to now (no rewind) it is refused. A `view_tick` 2 s old is
  clamped and logged.
- `server/hostile_test.odin` (UDP):
  - every step of the hostile script is refused or dropped and logged with its expected
    violation;
  - the hostile client is kicked by the step whose weight crosses 100, before the script
    ends;
  - an honest bot connected meanwhile sees no correction and no disconnect;
  - the server's tick p99 over the run stays under `TICK_DURATION`.
- `netcode` proxy tests: every transport attack above is refused and counted. The link and
  the host stay up, except the targeted connection for the forged-token case, which is
  untouched.

**Exit check:**

- Tampered, replayed and forged packets are refused and counted (the proxy tests).
- `test_account_proof_bound` passes.
- The hostile client is refused, logged and kicked (`hostile_test`). Honest play stays clean
  (`test_honest_play_clean`).
- The lag-compensation test passes. Every UDP test passes encrypted.
- CI green on the three OSes, the Wireshark step decoding the plaintext capture with no
  malformed packet.

**Check by hand:**

- A LAN game, Linux host and a Windows or macOS client: join, play ten minutes, look at the
  host's capture in Wireshark with the dissector.
- Change the host's `server.key`: the client refuses with the message, and joins again after
  its line is removed.
