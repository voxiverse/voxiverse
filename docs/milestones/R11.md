# R11: the frame graph and an HDR target

Arc rules: [R.md](R.md#rules-for-every-r-milestone). One session; not split (#87: the HDR
target is a declaration once the graph exists). R11b and R11c build the look on it.

**Why.** Every pass draws into the 8-bit sRGB output inside rendering scopes the frame opens
by hand, and every barrier between passes is written by hand where the pass is
(`begin_frame`'s depth barrier, `image_barrier`, R7's depth transitions, `world_upload`'s and
`cull_dispatch`'s memory barriers, `offscreen_begin`, `screenshot_record`). There is nowhere to
put post-processing, half-resolution passes or intermediate images, and each new pass needs
barriers someone must get right.

**Checked against the code (2026-10-30, #87; corrections to the old R11 text):**

- What the frame is after R7-R10: cull phase 1 (compute) → rendering A (world) → the depth
  pyramid (compute) → cull phase 2 (compute) → rendering B (world late, entities, held, sky,
  water, debug, the view model after `clear_depth`, UI) → the offscreen copy and the
  screenshot (`end_frame`); uploads on the transfer queue (R10) with a semaphore wait. The
  preview (`client/preview.odin`, a render-to-texture of the player, `gpu/target.odin`) records
  before the frame's rendering.
- The UI must stay out of the HDR target: it is drawn after the tonemap, into the output, so
  R11b's dither and grade never touch it.
- Screenshots and `images` read the presented (or offscreen) image after the UI: unchanged
  by R11, which keeps the output's format.
- **Barriers inside one pass's own buffers** (`cull_dispatch`: its staging copies before its
  dispatch) stay in the pass: the graph orders passes and the resources they share, not a
  pass's private steps.
- "Images are transient, allocated from R9's allocator and aliased": the frame's transients
  today are the HDR colour target, the depth image and R7's pyramid, all alive for most of
  the frame, so aliasing has little to share until R11b's luminance pass and R12's cloud
  target; it is built and tested now, used by them.

## Design (R11)

### The graph (`client/gpu/graph.odin`): declare, compile, then run in order

No callbacks (the ground rules' lesson from the prototype's `rawptr` callbacks): the frame
declares its passes, the graph compiles them, and the frame then runs them one by one with
`graph_begin_pass` / `graph_end_pass`, which emit the compiled barriers and open and close the
rendering scope.

- **Resources.** `graph_import_image(g, image, layout_now, name)` (the output: swapchain or
  offscreen image; the preview's target), `graph_import_buffer` (the cull commands, the
  arena, the visibility bits: what passes share), `graph_transient_image(g, desc, name)`
  (format, size or a fraction of the output's, mips, usage) → `Graph_Image`.
- **Passes**, in declaration order (no reordering: the frame's order is the code's):
  `graph_pass(g, name, kind: .Graphics | .Compute | .Transfer)`; uses:
  `graph_color(pass, image, .Clear(color) | .Load)`, `graph_depth(pass, image, .Clear(0) |
  .Load, write: bool)`, `graph_sample(pass, image)`, `graph_storage(pass, image, .Read |
  .Write | .Read_Write)`, `graph_buffer(pass, buffer, stages, access)`, `graph_indirect(pass,
  buffer)`, `graph_transfer_src`/`_dst`.
- **Compile** (`graph_compile(g) -> bool`, pure over the declarations, no Vulkan calls): for
  each resource, the last access (stages, accesses, layout, queue); before each pass, one
  `vk.DependencyInfo` of image and memory barriers for every hazard (read after write, write
  after read, write after write, a layout change), merged per pass; the first use of a
  transient is from `UNDEFINED` (its contents discarded); the output ends in
  `PRESENT_SRC_KHR` or `TRANSFER_SRC_OPTIMAL` for the offscreen copy. Errors are values (a pass
  reading a transient nothing wrote; an attachment of the wrong usage), logged and the frame
  skipped, never a validation-layer surprise.
- **Aliasing.** Transient images' lifetimes (first to last pass using them) are intervals;
  images whose intervals do not overlap and whose memory requirements fit share one R9
  allocation (greedy by size, largest first), each bound at offset 0 of it
  (`VK_IMAGE_CREATE_ALIAS_BIT` not needed: no two are alive at once, each starts from
  `UNDEFINED`). Transients are made when their description changes (the output's size), not
  per frame: the compile result is cached by a hash of the declarations, so a steady frame
  compiles nothing but the barrier list.
- **Run.** `graph_begin_pass(g, cmd, pass)` emits its barriers, opens `vkCmdBeginRendering`
  for a graphics pass from its attachments (viewport and scissor at the attachment's size) and
  a debug label with the pass's name (so `GPU_SECTION_METRICS` names stay: `world`, `sky`,
  ...); `graph_end_pass` closes them. A pass with nothing to draw still runs (its clears).
- Hand-written barriers removed: `begin_frame`'s depth barrier and colour transition,
  `image_barrier`'s callers, R7's depth transitions, `offscreen_begin`, the barriers before
  and after the screenshot and present copies (those become `.Transfer` passes), and the
  memory barriers between the cull, pyramid and draw passes. Kept inside their pass:
  `cull_dispatch`'s staging copies before its dispatch, `world_upload`'s growth copy
  ordering on the transfer queue (R10's own submission, outside the graph).

### The HDR target and the tonemap

- The scene renders into `scene_color`, a transient `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` image at the
  output's size (colour attachment and sampled; `COLOR_ATTACHMENT` and `SAMPLED_IMAGE` for
  this format are mandatory in Vulkan 1.0). Every scene pipeline (world, water, entities,
  held, sky, debug) is created for that format (`Pipeline_Desc.color_format`); the UI's stays
  the output's.
- **`tonemap`** (`assets/shaders/tonemap.vert`/`.frag`, a full-screen triangle): reads
  `scene_color` with `texelFetch` (one texel per output pixel, no filtering) and writes
  `clamp(c, 0, 1)`; the output's sRGB format encodes as the old passes' writes did. Label
  `tonemap`, metric `gpu_tonemap`.
- The frame: `cull` → `world` (A: `scene_color` cleared, depth cleared) → `depth pyramid` →
  `cull` (late) → `world` (B: load) + entities, held, sky, water, debug, view model (one
  graphics pass, as today; `clear_depth` inside it stays) → `tonemap` (into the output) →
  `ui` (load the output) → `present copy` / `screenshot` (transfer passes when wanted).
- The benchmark's offscreen image is the graph's output (its size is the output's), so
  `gpu_present_copy` keeps meaning the copy onto the window.

## The order of the steps (R11)

1. `graph.odin`'s declarations, compile and aliasing as pure code; its tests (no device).
2. The run side; the current passes declared (no HDR yet); every hand-written inter-pass
   barrier removed; `images` (unchanged), every `--check` under synchronization validation.
3. `scene_color`, the pipelines' formats, `tonemap`; `images` unchanged with no re-accept.
4. Budgets, measurement, docs (debugging.md: the graph, how to add a pass, how to read a
   compile error; Graphics: the HDR target), history.md.

## Tests (R11)

- `client/gpu/graph_test.odin` (no device; barriers compared as values):
  - a colour attachment written then sampled: one image barrier `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OUTPUT` /
    `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_WRITE` → `FRAGMENT_SHADER` / `SHADER_SAMPLED_READ`, layout
    `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL` → `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL`;
  - depth written in one pass, sampled by compute, loaded and written by the next graphics
    pass (R7's pattern): the two transitions with the exact stages;
  - a compute storage write then an indirect read (the cull pattern): one memory barrier
    `COMPUTE_SHADER` / `SHADER_STORAGE_WRITE` → `DRAW_INDIRECT` / `INDIRECT_COMMAND_READ`;
  - write after read and write after write on a buffer; no barrier between two reads in the
    same layout;
  - two transients with disjoint lifetimes share an allocation, overlapping ones never; a
    transient's first barrier is from `UNDEFINED`;
  - a pass reading a transient nothing wrote is a compile error; the compile of a steady
    frame is cached (the hash path).
- The device's test is synchronization validation: every `--check` (`cull`, `hiz`,
  `occlusion`, `quads`, `arena`, `uploads`, `blast`, `fly`, `tour`), the smoke benchmarks
  and `images` run under it in CI; a barrier deliberately dropped from a compiled list is
  tried once and must be reported by the layer (said in the report).
- `images`: every scene unchanged, **no re-accept** (pictures and counts); this is the
  gate that the HDR path is the identity.
- `build bench budgets -check`: `budget_graph` (`tools/bench/budgets.odin`): declaring and
  compiling R11's frame (12 passes) 100 000 times uncached; `Graph_Compile` mean at most
  20 us.

## Benchmarks (R11)

`bench compare <the commit before R11> views`, `views -size:2560x1440`, `flight`:
`gpu_frame` on `views` within 5% (the old target: the HDR target's bandwidth plus the tonemap);
`gpu_tonemap` reported at both sizes; `frame_record` within noise (the graph's CPU cost);
`flight` frame p99 within noise.

## Left out (R11)

Reordering passes, async compute, render-pass merging for tilers, a graph across frames
(history buffers: none yet), the preview's own graph (it stays `gpu/target.odin`, imported as a
resource of its own pass). Any change to the look (R11b, R11c).

## Exit check (R11)

**Gated in CI:** the graph tests; every `--check` and the smoke benchmarks clean under
synchronization validation; `images` passes with no re-accept; `build bench budgets -check`
within `Graph_Compile`; `build check` and the suite. **Measured** (development machine,
reported): `views` `gpu_frame` within 5% of the commit before R11.
